## Goal

By the end of this lesson, you can add a picture and a shape to a slide, and resize and move them.

## Why It Matters

People remember a picture better than a long line of words. A mobile shop can show a photo of
its new phone next to the price. A school can show a photo of last year's sports day. A shape
with the word **New** catches the eye, like a bright sticker on a shop window. You
already did this in Word in [Lesson 4.10](../module-04-ms-word/pictures-and-shapes.mdx).
PowerPoint works in a very similar way.

## Key Terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|------|----------------|
| Picture file | A photo or drawing saved on the computer, like a photo kept in an album. |
| This Device | The choice under **Pictures** for a picture saved on your own computer. |
| Sizing handle | A small mark on the edge or corner of a selected picture or shape. You drag it to change the size. |
| Resize | To make a picture or shape bigger or smaller. |
| Four-headed arrow | The mouse pointer with four arrow points. When you see it, you can drag the object to a new place. |
| Shape | A ready-made drawing, such as a circle, square, star or arrow. |

You learned to drag in [Lesson 4.10](../module-04-ms-word/pictures-and-shapes.mdx): hold down the
left mouse button, move the mouse, then let go.

## Step by Step

You need one picture file saved on your computer, for example a photo of a phone or a shop.
Find out which folder it is in before you start.

Open PowerPoint and start a blank presentation (see [Lesson 5.1](./starting-powerpoint.mdx)).
You will make one slide for Hamza Mobile Centre.

1. Click inside **Click to add title** and type **Hamza Mobile Centre**.
2. Click inside the box below the title and type **New phones this month**. This line tells
   customers what the slide is about.

**Inserting a picture**

3. Select the **Insert** tab. *You see the Insert tab buttons.*
4. In the **Images** group, select **Pictures**, then **This Device**. **This Device** means a
   picture saved on your own computer. *The **Insert Picture** box opens. It shows your
   **Pictures** folder.*
5. Go to the folder where your picture is saved. Select the picture file.
6. Select **Insert**. *The picture appears on the slide, and it is selected.* It may be very
   big and cover your words. You fix that next.

**Resizing the picture**

7. Click the picture, so that it is selected. *You see sizing handles on its edges and
   corners.*
8. Hold down **Shift**. Drag a corner handle towards the middle of the picture. *The picture
   gets smaller.* **Shift** keeps the picture's shape, so the phone does not look stretched or
   squashed.
9. Make the picture about as big as a quarter of the slide. A small, clear picture leaves room
   for your words.

**Moving the picture**

10. Point to the edge of the picture. *The pointer becomes a four-headed arrow.* This arrow
    means "you can move this now".
11. Drag the picture to the lower-right part of the slide. *The picture moves.* Now the title
    and the line below it are clear to read.

{/* Screenshot to add: A slide in PowerPoint 2021 with a selected picture, showing its sizing handles on the edges and corners. */}

**Adding a shape**

A shape with a word inside it points people to the most important news.

12. Click an empty part of the slide, so that the picture is no longer selected.
13. Select the **Insert** tab, then **Shapes**. *You see a gallery of shapes.*
14. Select a shape you like, for example a circle or a box.
15. Click on an empty part of the slide and drag. *The shape appears as you drag.* Hold
    **Shift** while you drag if you want a perfect circle or square.
16. With the shape still selected, type **New**. *The word appears inside the shape.* Now the
    shape tells customers that the phone is new.
17. To move the shape, point to its edge until you see the four-headed arrow, then drag. Put it
    near the picture.
18. Save the presentation as **Mobile Centre** (see [Lesson 5.3](./saving-and-slide-show.mdx)),
    so that your work is kept.

Use one or two pictures on a slide, not many. Too many pictures make a slide crowded.

## Practice

Your neighbour runs Sadia's Home Bakery. She wants one slide to show at a school fair. Use a
picture file saved on your computer, such as a photo of a cake or of food.

1. Open PowerPoint and select **Blank Presentation**.
2. Type the title **Sadia's Home Bakery**. In the box below it, type **Fresh cakes every day**.
3. Insert your picture file (**Insert**, **Pictures**, **This Device**). Go to the folder where
   it is saved, select it, and select **Insert**.
4. Hold **Shift** and drag a corner handle to make the picture smaller. This keeps the picture's
   shape.
5. Point to the edge of the picture and drag it to a corner of the slide, so that it does not
   cover the words.
6. Insert a shape (**Insert**, **Shapes**). Drag on the slide to draw it.
7. With the shape selected, type **Order Now**. This tells people what to do.
8. Move the shape so that it does not cover the picture or the title.
9. Save the presentation as **Bakery Slide**.
10. Check your work: the slide shows the title **Sadia's Home Bakery** and the line **Fresh
    cakes every day**. Your picture is smaller than before, not stretched, and does not cover
    the words. One shape has the words **Order Now** inside it. The title bar shows **Bakery
    Slide**.

## Common Mistakes

- **The picture looks stretched or squashed** → Press **Ctrl+Z** to undo. Then hold **Shift**
  and drag a corner handle, not a handle on the side.
- **The picture covers the title** → Make the picture smaller, then drag it to an empty part of
  the slide.
- **The word does not go inside the shape** → Click the shape to select it first. Then type.
- **A shape is in the wrong place or not needed** → To move it, drag its edge. To remove it,
  select it and press **Delete**.
- **Not finding the picture file** → Think about where the picture was saved, and go to that
  folder in the **Insert Picture** box.
