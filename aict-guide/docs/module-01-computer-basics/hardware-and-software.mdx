## Goal

By the end of this lesson, you can tell whether something is hardware or software.

## Why It Matters

A computer is made of two kinds of things. Some parts you can touch. Other things you cannot
touch, but they tell the computer what to do. A shop that prints bills needs a printer, and it
needs a program to make the bill. A school office needs a keyboard and a screen, and an app to
type result cards. When you know the difference, you can say clearly what a computer needs.

Think of a piano and music. The piano is the thing you can touch. The music is what it plays.
A computer is the same. The hardware is like the piano. The software is like the music.

## Step by Step

Sit at your computer. Keep this page open. You will find hardware and software on your own
computer.

1. Touch the keyboard, the mouse and the screen, one by one. These are **hardware**. You can
   touch them, so they are hardware.
2. Look for a printer and speakers near your computer. If you see them, they are hardware too.
3. Look at the screen. The computer is running **software** now. Software is a set of
   instructions. You cannot touch it. You only see what it does on the screen.
4. Learn three examples of software, so that you can name them.
   - **Windows**: the main software that makes the computer work.
   - **Paint**: an app for drawing pictures.
   - **Microsoft Edge**: an app for opening web pages. It is called a web browser.
5. Think about this book. If you are reading it on a computer, it is open in a web browser.
   The web browser is software.
6. Remember the piano and the music. The piano is like hardware. The music is like software.
   This comparison helps you remember which is which.
7. Hardware follows the instructions in the software. Software needs hardware to run on. So a
   computer needs both of them.
8. Look at the diagram above. Find Hardware, then Software, then the arrow between them. The
   arrow shows that they work together.

## Practice

Sana is opening a small tuition centre. She makes a list of what her new computer needs. Sort
her list into hardware and software. You need one sheet of paper and a pen.

1. Draw two boxes on your paper. Write **Hardware** in one box and **Software** in the other.
2. Read Sana's list: keyboard, mouse, Windows, screen, Paint, printer, Microsoft Edge,
   speakers.
3. Write each thing in the right box. Ask yourself: "Can I touch it?" If yes, it is hardware.
4. Go to your computer. Touch each piece of hardware from the list that you can find. This
   shows you that hardware is real and solid.
5. Check your work: **Hardware** has keyboard, mouse, screen, printer and speakers (5 things).
   **Software** has Windows, Paint and Microsoft Edge (3 things). You touched at least the
   keyboard, the mouse and the screen on your computer.

## Common Mistakes

- **Thinking software is something you can hold** → Software is instructions. You cannot touch
  it.
- **Thinking hardware can work alone** → Hardware follows the instructions in the software. A
  computer needs both.
- **Calling every part "the computer"** → Each part has its own name. In the next lesson, you
  learn the names of the parts.
