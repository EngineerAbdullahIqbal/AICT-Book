## Goal

By the end of this lesson, you can turn one crowded slide into clear slides with few words, large text and one idea each.

## Why It Matters

Slides should help people listen to you. If a slide is full of sentences, people read the screen
and stop listening. If the text is small, people at the back cannot read it at all. Think of a shop owner
showing new phones, a health worker at a Basic Health Unit, or a college student with a project.
They all need slides that people can read in a moment, so the listeners stay with the speaker.


**Diagram in words:** Start with the crowded slide and find the ideas in it. Give each idea its
own slide, with a few key words in large text and a picture that helps. The result is clear
slides.

## Key Terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|------|----------------|
| Crowded slide | A slide with too many words, pictures or ideas on it. |
| Key words | The few most important words of a sentence. They remind you of the full idea. |
| One idea per slide | A rule for slides: each slide shows only one point. |
| Font size | How big the letters are. A bigger number means bigger letters. |
| Contrast | How different the text colour is from the background colour. High contrast is clear to read. |
| Relevant picture | A picture that shows what you are talking about, such as a phone on a phone shop slide. |

Your slides are like the signboard of a shop. People read a signboard in a moment, from across
the road. Your talk is like the shopkeeper who explains the details. These rules help your slides
work like a good signboard:

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| Few words: short lines, about 25 words or fewer on a slide | People listen to you instead of reading the screen. |
| Large text: 18 or bigger, and 24 or more is better | People can read it from far away. Use bigger text in a big room. |
| One idea per slide | Each idea has room, and people follow one point at a time. |
| A picture that explains, not many pictures | A relevant picture helps people learn. Too many pictures confuse them. |
| A calm theme with high contrast | The background does not pull eyes away from your words. |

You learned to make slides in Module 5. This lesson uses those skills to make slides that help
your talk. These ideas work the same when you speak in Urdu.

## Step by Step

Open PowerPoint and start a blank presentation (see
[Lesson 5.1](../module-05-ms-powerpoint/starting-powerpoint.mdx)). Make one **Title and Content**
slide (see [Lesson 5.2](../module-05-ms-powerpoint/adding-slides-and-text.mdx)). Type this
title and text. It is the crowded slide you will fix.

```text
Title: Our Repair Service

Text: Hamza Mobile Centre now repairs all kinds of mobile phones. We can
change broken screens, change batteries that do not last long, and fix
charging ports that are loose. Most repairs are ready on the same day, so
you do not have to wait long. Every repair has a 3-month warranty from our
shop, so you can trust our work.
```

**Look at the crowded slide**

1. Select the **Slide Show** tab, then select **From Beginning**. *The slide fills the screen.*
   You now see the slide the way your audience will see it.
2. Stand up and walk a few steps back from the screen. Try to read the text. *The letters are
   small, and there are many lines.* People at the back of a room would see the same thing.
3. Press **Esc** to end the slide show. *You see the PowerPoint window again.*
4. Count the words in the text box. *There are about 60 words.* That is more than twice the
   25-word guide.

**Find the ideas**

5. On a sheet of paper, write each idea in the text in a few words. *You find three ideas: what
   we repair, ready the same day, and a 3-month warranty.* Each idea will get its own slide.

**One idea, a few key words**

6. Change the title to **We Repair Phones**. A title that says the idea helps listeners know the
   point at once.
7. Delete the long text. Type these three short lines instead, pressing **Enter** after each:
   **Broken screens**, **Weak batteries**, **Loose charging ports**. Short lines are safer,
   because some projectors cut off the edges of a slide.
8. Click in the Notes pane below the slide. Type the full sentence: **We can change broken
   screens and weak batteries, and fix loose charging ports.** The slide shows key words for the
   audience. The notes keep the full sentence for you (see
   [Lesson 5.11](../module-05-ms-powerpoint/speaker-notes-and-printing.mdx)).

   If you do not see the Notes pane, select **Notes** on the bar at the bottom of the PowerPoint
   window.

9. On the **Home** tab, select the arrow by **New Slide**, then select **Title and Content**.
   *A new slide appears.* Type the title **Ready the Same Day** and the text **Most repairs**.
   In its notes, type: **Most repairs are ready on the same day.**
10. Add one more **Title and Content** slide in the same way. Type the title **3-Month Warranty**
    and the text **On every repair**. In its notes, type: **Every repair has a 3-month warranty
    from our shop.**

*You now have three slides. Each has one idea and only a few words.*

**Make the text large**

11. Go to slide 1. Select the three short lines. In the **Font Size** box on the **Home** tab,
    type **28** and press **Enter**. *The words become bigger.* Text smaller than 18 is hard to
    read from far away (see [Lesson 5.4](../module-05-ms-powerpoint/formatting-text-on-slides.mdx)).
12. Do the same on slides 2 and 3. Text the same size on every slide looks neat.

**Add a picture that helps, and a calm theme**

13. If you have a picture of a phone on your computer, add it to slide 1 (see
    [Lesson 5.6](../module-05-ms-powerpoint/pictures-and-shapes.mdx)). One picture that shows
    your idea helps people understand. Do not add pictures only to fill space.
14. Choose a calm theme where the text is clear to read (see
    [Lesson 5.5](../module-05-ms-powerpoint/design-themes.mdx)). High contrast between text and
    background helps people read.

**Check from far away**

15. Start the slide show from the beginning again. Walk a few steps back. *The words are large,
    and each slide has only a few of them.* Press **Esc** at the end.
16. Read every word on your slides once more, to check the spelling. Correct spelling helps your
    audience respect you and your work.
17. Look at slide 1 and say its full sentence aloud, without reading it from the notes. Do the same
    for slides 2 and 3. *You hear yourself explain each idea while the slide shows only key words.*
18. Save the presentation as **Repair Service** (see
    [Lesson 5.3](../module-05-ms-powerpoint/saving-and-slide-show.mdx)), so that your work is
    kept.

## Practice

Here is a crowded slide for a computer centre. Fix it so that it helps the speaker.

```text
Title: Sunrise Computer Centre

Text: Sunrise Computer Centre is starting a new computer course on
1 November. You will learn typing, MS Word, MS Excel and MS PowerPoint.
Lessons are from Monday to Friday, from 4 pm to 6 pm. The fee is Rs 3,000
a month, and the first week is free for every new student.
```

1. Open PowerPoint and make one **Title and Content** slide with this title and text.
2. Start the slide show and stand a few steps back. Notice how hard the slide is to read. Press
   **Esc**.
3. On paper, write the ideas in the text in a few words. You should find three: what you learn,
   when, and the fee.
4. Give each idea its own **Title and Content** slide, with a short title that says the idea.
5. On each slide, type only a few key words, one short line each. Keep each slide well under 25
   words.
6. Type the full sentences in the Notes pane of each slide, so that you remember the details.
7. Make the key words font size **28** on every slide.
8. If you have a relevant picture, such as a computer, add it to one slide. Choose a calm theme.
9. Start the slide show from the beginning and check it from a few steps back. Press **Esc** at
   the end.
10. Explain each slide aloud in your own words, to a family member or a friend. If no one is free,
    record yourself on a phone and listen.
11. Save the presentation as **Sunrise Course**.
12. Check your work: tick each one. ☐ Each slide has one idea and a title that says it. ☐ No slide
    has more than 25 words. ☐ Every line fits on one line. ☐ The text is size 28 and clear to read
    from a few steps back. ☐ The full sentences are in the notes, not on the slides. ☐ I explained
    each slide without reading it aloud.

## Check Questions

1. What is the smallest font size Microsoft suggests for slides? When should you go bigger?

   <details>
   <summary>Show answer</summary>

   **Answer:** Try not to use text smaller than 18. Go bigger in a large room, where the audience
   is far away. Size 24 or more is better.

   </details>

2. Write this slide line again in a few key words: "Our shop is open every day of the week, from
   10 in the morning until 9 at night."

   <details>
   <summary>Show answer</summary>

   **Answer:** For example: **Open every day, 10 am to 9 pm.** Your words may be a little
   different. The line should be short and fit on one line.

   </details>

3. Why should you not put your whole talk on your slides?

   <details>
   <summary>Show answer</summary>

   **Answer:** People would read the screen instead of listening to you. The slide text should
   remind you of your points. You give the full message when you speak.

   </details>

## Common Mistakes

- **Whole sentences and paragraphs on a slide** → Use a few key words. Put the full sentences in
  the Notes pane.
- **Making the text small to fit more words** → Keep the text 18 or bigger, and 24 or more if you
  can. If it does not fit, the slide has too much on it. Make another slide.
- **Adding many pictures to make the slide look nice** → Use one picture that shows your idea.
- **Reading the slides aloud** → Use the slide words as reminders. Look at your listeners and
  explain in your own words.
- **A bright, busy background** → Choose a calm theme with high contrast, so people look at your
  words.
