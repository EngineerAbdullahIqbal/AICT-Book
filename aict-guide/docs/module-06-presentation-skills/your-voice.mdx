## Goal

By the end of this lesson, you can speak loudly, clearly and at a steady speed, with pauses, and practise aloud.

## Why It Matters

Your voice carries your message. At a job interview, the people in front of you must hear every
answer. A shop owner telling customers about a new service must speak so the whole shop hears.
A health worker at a Basic Health Unit may speak to a full room. If your voice is too soft or too
fast, people miss what you say. These voice skills work the same when you speak in Urdu.

**Diagram in words:** A clear voice has four parts: loud enough, a steady speed, pauses, and
clear words. When you use all four, your listeners understand you.

## Key Terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|------|----------------|
| Volume | How loud or soft your voice is. |
| Project your voice | Speak loudly enough that people at the back of the room can hear you, without shouting. |
| Pace | How fast or slow you speak. |
| Pause | A short stop in your talk, when you say nothing for a moment. |
| Pronounce | Say a word in the correct way, with all its sounds. |
| Filler words | Sounds like "um", "uh" and "like" that people say when they stop to think. |
| Monotone | A flat voice that stays the same all the time. It sounds dull to listeners. |

Think of a cricket commentator on the radio. You cannot see them, but you follow every ball. They
speak clearly, change their speed, and stop at the right moments. You can learn to do the same.

## Step by Step

You will practise with a short talk. Bilal Ahmed is at a job interview. He introduces himself.
All the names in this lesson are made up.

:::note[Sample talk]

Good morning. My name is Bilal Ahmed. I live in Multan. I finished my AICT computer course this
year. I can type quickly and make documents in Word. I can also make tables and bills in Excel.
I am careful with numbers, and I check my work twice. I like working with people. In my uncle's
shop, I helped customers every day. I want this job because I enjoy learning new things. Thank
you for meeting me.

:::

**Get ready**

1. Copy the sample talk onto a sheet of paper. Leave space between the lines. You will add marks
   to it later.
2. Read it once silently, so that you know all the words before you speak.
3. Stand up. Read it aloud once in your normal voice. *You hear how you sound now.* Later you
   can compare.

**Volume**

4. Choose a spot at the far side of the room, such as the door or a far wall. Read the first two
   sentences again, so that a person standing there could hear you. This is called projecting your
   voice. It is louder than talking to a friend, but it is not shouting.
5. Read the next sentence. Listen to its last word. Many people get quieter at the end of a
   sentence. Keep the same volume to the last word, so that listeners do not miss it. *You hear
   the last word as clearly as the first.*

**Speed**

6. Open the clock or stopwatch on your phone, or look at a watch. Read the whole talk aloud and
   time yourself. The talk has about 80 words. At a comfortable speed, it takes about one minute.
7. If you finished much faster, read it again more slowly. People often speak fast when they are
   nervous. Slower speech gives listeners time to understand.

**Pauses**

8. On your paper, draw a slash mark / after every sentence. A slash shows where you will pause and
   take a breath. A breath after each sentence also stops you from speeding up.
9. Choose two or three important words. Write them in capital letters. Pause for a moment before
   an important word, so that listeners notice it. *Your paper now looks like this:*

   ```text
   Good morning. / My name is Bilal Ahmed. / I live in Multan. /
   I finished my AICT computer course this year. /
   I can type QUICKLY and make documents in Word. /
   I can also make tables and bills in EXCEL. /
   I am careful with numbers, and I check my work TWICE. / ...
   ```

10. Read the talk aloud again. Stop and breathe at every slash. Say the capital words a little
    louder and with feeling. Then your voice is not flat, and listeners hear what matters most.

**Clear words**

11. Read the talk once with very big mouth movements. Open your mouth wide and say every sound.
    It feels strange, but it trains your mouth to make each word clear. Then read it again in a
    normal way. *Your words sound clearer than before.*
12. Find words that are hard for you, such as names, places and numbers. Say each one aloud three
    times, so that you pronounce it correctly in the real talk.
13. Listen for "um" and "uh" as you read. When you need a moment to think, pause silently
    instead. A silent pause is better than a filler word.

**Practise aloud**

14. Read the whole talk aloud once more with all the skills: volume, speed, pauses and clear
    words. Practise aloud, not only in your head, because your mouth needs practice too.
15. If you can, record yourself on your phone and listen to it. Note what you did well and what
    to improve. This step is optional.
16. Ask a family member or a friend to sit at the far side of the room. Read the talk to them.
    Ask them two things: did you hear every word, and was the speed comfortable? Their answer
    tells you what to practise next.

If you make slides in PowerPoint, write your pause marks in the speaker notes. Then you see them
while you speak. [Lesson 5.11](../module-05-ms-powerpoint/speaker-notes-and-printing.mdx) shows
how to add speaker notes.

## Practice

Rashid owns Noor General Store. He is telling his customers about two changes at his shop. You
will give his talk.

:::note[Sample talk]

Good evening, everyone. Thank you for coming. I am Rashid from Noor General Store. I have two
pieces of good news for you. First, from next Monday, our shop opens at eight in the morning. You
can buy fresh milk and bread before work. Second, we now deliver to your home. Call us before six
in the evening. We bring your order the same day. Delivery is free for orders over one thousand
rupees. Thank you, and see you soon.

:::

1. Copy the talk onto paper, with space between the lines.
2. Draw a slash / after every sentence, so that you know where to pause and breathe.
3. Choose three important words, for example **eight**, **deliver** and **free**. Write them in
   capital letters, so that you remember to say them with feeling.
4. Say the hard words three times each: the shop name, the times and the numbers.
5. Read the talk aloud once with big mouth movements, then once in a normal way.
6. Time yourself. Aim for about one minute. If you are much faster, slow down and pause longer.
7. Read the talk to a family member or a friend at the far side of the room. You can also record
   yourself on your phone and listen.
8. Check your work: tick each one. ☐ Your listener heard every word, even at the end of each
   sentence. ☐ The talk took about one minute. ☐ You paused at every slash. ☐ You said the three
   capital words with feeling. ☐ You paused silently instead of saying "um" or "uh".

## Check Questions

1. Take the first three sentences of Rashid's talk. Mark where you will pause, then say them
   aloud. Where did you pause, and why?

   <details>
   <summary>Show answer</summary>

   **Answer:** Pause after each sentence: after "everyone", after "coming" and after "Store".
   A pause lets you take a breath. It also gives listeners time to understand each idea.

   </details>

2. Why should you speak louder in a talk than when you chat with a friend?

   <details>
   <summary>Show answer</summary>

   **Answer:** So that everyone can hear you, even the people at the back of the room. This is
   called projecting your voice. It is louder than chatting, but it is not shouting.

   </details>

3. You often say "um" when you stop to think. What can you do instead?

   <details>
   <summary>Show answer</summary>

   **Answer:** Pause silently for a moment. Practise the talk aloud several times, so that you
   know what comes next and need fewer stops to think.

   </details>

## Common Mistakes

- **Your voice gets quieter at the end of each sentence** → Keep the same volume to the last
  word. Ask a listener at the back of the room to check.
- **Speaking too fast because you are nervous** → Time yourself. Take a breath at every slash
  mark, so that you slow down.
- **Filling every silence with "um" or "uh"** → Pause silently instead. Practise until you know
  what comes next.
- **Practising only in your head** → Say the talk aloud, even to an empty room. Your mouth needs
  practice too.
- **Using the same flat voice all the time** → Mark the important words and say them with
  feeling.
