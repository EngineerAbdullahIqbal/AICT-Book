## Goal

By the end of this lesson, you can give a table a centred title, a bold coloured heading row and borders.

## Why It Matters

A plain list of words and numbers is hard to read. A shop price list with a clear title, bold
headings and neat lines looks tidy. A school result card is clearer when its headings stand out.
Customers and parents can find what they need quickly. A tidy table also looks good when you
print it.

## Key Terms

| Term | Simple meaning |
|------|----------------|
| Format | To change how cells look, not what is in them. It is like dressing up the same words. |
| Bold | Thick, dark letters. The keys are **Ctrl+B**. |
| Fill colour | A colour inside a cell, behind the text, like a highlighter on paper. |
| Border | A line you add around cells, like a line drawn with a ruler. Borders print on paper. |
| Gridlines | The light lines you see around every cell. They do not print unless you change a setting. |
| Alignment | Where the text sits in a cell: left, centre or right. |
| Merge & Center | A button that joins cells into one big cell and puts the text in the middle. |

## Step by Step

Follow these steps on your own computer. Open the **Shop List** workbook from
[Lesson 7.3](./rows-and-columns.mdx). To select many cells, click the first cell and drag to the
last one. Formatting works much like in Word. See
[Lesson 4.5: Formatting Text](../module-04-ms-word/formatting-text.mdx).

**A title for the table**

A title tells the reader what the table is about.

1. Right-click the row number **1** and select **Insert**. This makes space for a title above the
   headings. *You see an empty row 1. The headings move to row 2.*
2. Click cell A1. Type **Hamza General Store** and press **Enter**.
3. Drag from A1 to C1 to select the three cells.
4. On the **Home** tab, select **Merge & Center**. This puts the title in the middle, above the
   whole table. *You see A1 to C1 become one cell, with the title in the middle.*
5. On the **Home** tab, in the **Font** group, select **Bold**. Then select **Font Size** and choose
   **16**. A big, bold title stands out at the top of the page. *You see a bigger, bold title.*

**The heading row**

Bold, coloured headings look different from the items below them.

6. Drag from A2 to C2 to select the headings. Press **Ctrl+B**.
   *You see the headings become bold.*
7. On the **Home** tab, in the **Font** group, select the arrow next to **Fill Color**. Under
   **Theme Colors** or **Standard Colors**, select a light colour. A light colour makes the row
   stand out, and you can still read the text. *You see the heading cells filled with colour.*
8. On the **Home** tab, in the **Alignment** group, select **Center**. This puts each heading in
   the middle of its column. *You see the headings in the middle of their cells.*

**Borders**

9. Look at the light grey lines on the sheet. They are gridlines, and they do not print. Borders
   do print. So add borders if you want lines on paper.
10. Drag from A2 to the last price in column C.
11. On the **Home** tab, in the **Font** group, select the arrow next to **Borders**. Select
    **All Borders**. *You see a line around every cell in the table.*
12. Press **Ctrl+S** to save, so that your formatting is kept.

## Practice

Imran wants to print his stock list and put it up in his mobile shop. Make it neat. Use your
**Mobile Stock** workbook from the Practice in [Lesson 7.3](./rows-and-columns.mdx).

1. Insert a new row 1 above your headings, so that there is space for a title.
2. In A1, type a shop name, for example **Imran Mobile Centre**.
3. Select A1 to C1. Select **Merge & Center**.
4. Make the title bold and size 16.
5. Select the headings in row 2. Make them bold, give them a light fill colour and center them.
6. Select the whole table, from A2 to the last price in C8. Add **All Borders**, so that the lines
   print.
7. Press **Ctrl+S** to save.
8. Check your work: the title is big, bold and in the middle of A1 to C1. The headings in row 2
   are bold, coloured and centred. Every cell from A2 to C8 has a line around it.

## Check Questions

1. Show how you make the headings bold.
   **Answer:** Select the heading cells, then press Ctrl+B or select Bold on the Home tab.
2. What is the difference between gridlines and borders?
   **Answer:** Gridlines are the light lines around every cell, and they do not print. Borders are
   lines you add yourself, and they print.
3. You merge A1 to C1, but B1 also had some text in it. What happens to that text?
   **Answer:** It is deleted. Only the text in the top-left cell, A1, is kept.

## Common Mistakes

- **Formatting only one cell when you wanted the whole row** → Select all the cells first, then
  format them.
- **Typing parts of the title in B1 or C1 before merging** → Type the whole title in A1 only. Excel
  keeps only the top-left cell when it merges.
- **Choosing a dark fill colour, so the text is hard to read** → Choose a light colour. To remove a
  colour, select the arrow next to **Fill Color**, then **No Fill**.
- **Expecting the grey gridlines to print** → Add borders to the cells you want lines around.
