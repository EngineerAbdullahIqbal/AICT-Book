## 1. Module Summary

Module 5 taught how to *make* slides. This module teaches how to *present*: plan a short talk,
make slides that support it, speak clearly, use your body and eyes, control nerves, practise
with PowerPoint's timing tools, and answer questions. It ends with a real 3-minute presentation.

**Why it matters**: job interviews, shop meetings, school and college projects, NGO awareness
talks and freelancing client calls all need someone who can explain an idea clearly and calmly.

**Prerequisites**: Module 5 (PowerPoint 2021). The project uses the student's own slides.

**Sources** (constitution §3): speaking skills are general skills with no software maker, so
recognised education sources count: university pages (for example a university's public
speaking or communication centre), BBC Bitesize, GCFGlobal / learnfree.org, and Microsoft's
"Tips for creating and delivering an effective presentation". PowerPoint steps (Rehearse
Timings, Presenter View, Speaker Notes) come from Microsoft pages that apply to PowerPoint 2021.
Subscription-only tools (for example Speaker Coach) are not taught.

**How the lesson format fits a speaking skill**:
- *Step by Step* = a worked example: watching / reading a short sample talk and doing the steps
  yourself (planning on paper, saying lines aloud, timing yourself).
- *Practice* = a real-life speaking task done alone, with a family member or a friend, or by
  recording yourself on a phone (optional). "Check your work" is a short checklist (for example
  "I looked up at the listener at least 3 times").
- No step needs internet.

## 2. Learning Outcomes

By the end of Module 6, the student can:

- **LO-1**: Say who the audience is and what one main message the talk must give.
- **LO-2**: Plan a short talk with a beginning, a middle (2–3 points) and an end.
- **LO-3**: Make slides that help the talk: few words, large text, one idea per slide.
- **LO-4**: Speak clearly: loud enough, not too fast, with pauses.
- **LO-5**: Use body language: stand straight, look at the audience, use open hands.
- **LO-6**: Use simple ways to control nervousness.
- **LO-7**: Practise a talk using speaker notes, Rehearse Timings and Presenter View.
- **LO-8**: Listen to a question, answer it briefly, and say "I will find out" when unsure.
- **LO-9**: Give a 3-minute presentation with slides and get feedback with a checklist.

## 4. Acceptance Criteria — All Lessons

The common lesson criteria (Module 1 spec §4, AC-C1 to AC-C11, book-spec v1.4), plus:
- **AC-S1**: Every speaking tip (voice, pace, eye contact, posture, nerves, questions, slide
  design) comes from a recognised source listed in Sources. No personal opinion presented as fact.
- **AC-S2**: Every PowerPoint step cites a Microsoft page that applies to PowerPoint 2021, or sits
  in an "If your screen looks different" box with a confirmed backup.
- **AC-S3**: Examples are real-life talks in Pakistan (introducing yourself at a job interview, a
  shop owner explaining a new product, a health-awareness talk, a school project). Made-up names;
  neutral and respectful.
- **AC-S4**: Practice ends with a "Check your work" checklist the student can tick alone.

## 5. Lessons in Detail (topics; facts verified when written)

- **6.1**: purpose, audience, one main message; what listeners remember (only if sourced).
- **6.2**: an opening that says what the talk is about; 2–3 main points; a closing that repeats
  the main message; planning on paper or in speaker notes.
- **6.3**: few words per slide, large readable text, one idea per slide, pictures that explain;
  links to Module 5 lessons; Microsoft's tips (for example font size) where they apply.
- **6.4**: volume, speed, pauses, clear words; practising aloud.
- **6.5**: standing, eye contact, hands, facing the audience not the screen.
- **6.6**: preparation, breathing and practice to calm nerves (only sourced methods).
- **6.7**: Speaker Notes (Module 5 Lesson 5.11), **Rehearse Timings**, **Presenter View**
  (PowerPoint 2021 pages only).
- **6.8**: listening to the whole question, repeating it if needed, short answers, saying you
  will find out.
- **6.9**: a 3-minute talk with 3–5 slides on a real topic; a feedback checklist for a listener.
