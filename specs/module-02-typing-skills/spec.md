## 1. Module Summary

Students learn to type with all ten fingers without looking at the keyboard ("touch typing").
They start with how to sit and where to put their hands, then learn the keys row by row, then
type real words and sentences, and finally measure their speed and accuracy.

**Why this module matters**: every later module (Word, Excel, internet, freelancing) needs
typing. A student who types 25 words a minute without looking finishes a job letter in a few
minutes instead of half an hour.

**Prerequisites**: Module 1, especially Lesson 1.6 (Keyboard Basics: key groups, Shift, Caps lock,
Enter, Spacebar, Backspace) and Lesson 1.7 (opening Notepad).

**Keyboard**: the standard English keyboard on the lab computers. Urdu typing is taught in
Module 8 (InPage Urdu), not here.

**Where students type** (owner decision, 2026-10-14):
- **TypingMaster 10/11** is installed on the lab computers. Lessons use its **Touch Typing
  Course** and **Typing Test** for practice. Every TypingMaster step comes from the official
  *TypingMaster 11 User Manual* (TypingMaster, Inc., version 11.0, 2022), read from the archived
  copy of typingmaster.com/typing-tutor/support/user-manual.html.
- Verified from that manual: the **Course Selection** screen; **Start Course** / **Continue
  Course**; the Touch Typing Course has 12 lessons and over 70 exercises; the key to press turns
  green, and two hands with a green dot show which finger to use; the **Typing Test** shows
  **Gross speed**, **Accuracy** and **Net speed** (speed after errors), in WPM or KPM depending
  on Settings.
- **Trial limit** (same manual): an unregistered copy is fully featured for 7 days, then only
  the first two lessons of the course can be opened. So every lesson also has **Notepad drills**
  on the page (Notepad: Lesson 1.7), which always work, with or without TypingMaster.
- If a TypingMaster 10 screen differs from the version 11 manual, the step goes in an "If your
  screen looks different" box and in the owner's private test list.

**Sources** (constitution §3): touch typing is a general skill with no software maker, so
recognised education sources count (for example GCFGlobal / learnfree.org, BBC Bitesize, or a
university page). Windows and Notepad steps come from Microsoft pages, as in Module 1.

## 2. Learning Outcomes

By the end of Module 2, the student can:

- **LO-1**: Sit correctly and place both hands on the home row keys.
- **LO-2**: Type the home row keys with the correct fingers without looking.
- **LO-3**: Type the top row keys with the correct fingers.
- **LO-4**: Type the bottom row keys with the correct fingers.
- **LO-5**: Type capital letters and common punctuation (full stop, comma, question mark).
- **LO-6**: Type numbers from the number row.
- **LO-7**: Type whole words and sentences accurately, without looking at the keys.
- **LO-8**: Measure their own typing speed (words per minute) and accuracy.
- **LO-9**: Type a short real-life text (a paragraph of a job letter) within a time goal.

## 4. Acceptance Criteria — All Lessons

The common criteria from Module 1 (AC-C1 to AC-C11, with book-spec v1.3), plus:
- **AC-T1**: Every finger-to-key rule (which finger presses which key) comes from a recognised
  source listed in Sources.
- **AC-T2**: Every Practice has typing drills on the page that the student types in Notepad
  (always available): short lines first, then words, then sentences. Where it helps, Practice
  also points to the matching part of TypingMaster's Touch Typing Course, without naming a
  lesson number or keys that the manual does not confirm. Drills use everyday Pakistani words and names written in
  English letters (for example "Lahore", "chai", "Ali", "rupees").
- **AC-T3**: Every lesson has a short "look-away" check: the student types a line without
  looking at the keys, and counts mistakes.
- **AC-T4**: Practice ends with "Check your work:" giving a clear target (for example "no more
  than 2 mistakes in 5 lines").

## 5. Lessons in Detail (topics; facts are verified when each lesson is written)

### 2.1 Sitting Right and the Home Row
- **In scope**: sitting position, hands and wrists, the home row (A S D F and J K L ;), the
  small bumps on F and J if a source confirms them, resting fingers lightly, thumbs on the
  Spacebar.
- **Diagram idea**: left hand on A S D F, right hand on J K L ;.

### 2.2 Typing the Home Row Keys
- **In scope**: each finger's home key, typing drills with home-row letters ("sad", "lad",
  "flask"), returning to the home row after each key.

### 2.3 The Top Row
- **In scope**: Q W E R T Y U I O P; which finger reaches each key; drills.

### 2.4 The Bottom Row
- **In scope**: Z X C V B N M , . /; which finger reaches each key; drills.

### 2.5 Capital Letters and Punctuation
- **In scope**: the Shift keys (opposite-hand Shift if the source says so), full stop, comma,
  question mark; one space after a full stop; drills with names and short sentences.
- **Builds on**: Lesson 1.6 (Shift, Caps lock).

### 2.6 The Number Row
- **In scope**: the numbers 1–0 on the top row and which finger presses each; drills with
  prices in rupees, phone-style numbers (made up), dates.
- **Out of scope**: the number pad (named only).

### 2.7 Typing Words and Sentences
- **In scope**: typing common words and full sentences without looking; fixing mistakes with
  Backspace (Lesson 1.6); keeping a steady rhythm.

### 2.8 Measuring Your Speed and Accuracy
- **In scope**: TypingMaster's **Typing Test**: Gross speed, Accuracy and Net speed (from the
  manual); the module goal is a **Net speed of 20 WPM**; recording progress in a small table.
  Backup without TypingMaster: time one minute in Notepad and count the words typed.

### 2.9 Project: Type a Real Paragraph
- **In scope**: typing a short job-letter paragraph (links to Word Lesson 4.12) in Notepad
  against a time goal, then checking mistakes.
